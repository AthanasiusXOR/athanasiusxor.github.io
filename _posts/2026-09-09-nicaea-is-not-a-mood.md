People borrow Nicaea as atmosphere. Stone, gold, a council, a word that sounds older than the quarter. The council was not atmosphere. It was a fight about whether the church would confess the Son as God or as something nearby.

> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."
> ~ **John 1:1**

## The word they would not give up

*Homoousios* means "of the same substance." The rival word, a single letter over, meant "of similar substance." Arius could praise the Son in almost every register a Christian wanted to hear and still deny that the Son is the one true God. The council refused the almost. The creed they produced, and the church later received in fuller form, is a document of exclusions. It says who God is by refusing the smaller versions.

I keep that letter in mind when I read a security architecture. Similar-to-secure is a product category. The agent is deployed, the policy is named, the dashboard is green, and the substance — the control that would actually stop the thing — was never of one being with the threat. Appearance of godliness, denying the power. Paul told Timothy to avoid such people. I write findings about such systems.

## The creed, so it is not a slogan

This is the confession the name is tied to. The bracket marks the Western addition, "and the Son," which the Eastern churches do not confess in the same place. I do not sand that history off to make a cleaner banner.

> We believe in one God,
> the Father almighty,
> maker of heaven and earth,
> of all things visible and invisible.
>
> And in one Lord Jesus Christ,
> the only Son of God,
> begotten from the Father before all ages,
> God from God, Light from Light,
> true God from true God,
> begotten, not made;
> of the same essence as the Father.
> Through him all things were made.
>
> For us and for our salvation
> he came down from heaven;
> he became incarnate by the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary,
> and was made human.
> He was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate;
> he suffered and was buried.
> The third day he rose again, according to the Scriptures.
> He ascended to heaven
> and is seated at the right hand of the Father.
> He will come again with glory
> to judge the living and the dead.
> His kingdom will never end.
>
> And we believe in the Holy Spirit,
> the Lord, the giver of life.
> He proceeds from the Father **[and the Son].**
> With the Father and the Son he is worshiped and glorified.
> He spoke through the prophets.
>
> We believe in one holy catholic and apostolic church.
> We affirm one baptism for the forgiveness of sins.
> We look forward to the resurrection of the dead,
> and to life in the world to come. Amen.

## What I will not do with it

I will not spend the creed as scenery. When an essay here reaches for a doctrine, the doctrine has to remain itself. A technical claim that only works while the verse is in the room is not yet a claim. The epistle uses theological names for the work on purpose. Those names are accountable to the things they name. They are not a coat of paint.

I will also not pretend the council makes me severe and other people soft. Nicaea was about worship and salvation. It binds me before it binds a design review. The use I make of it in this work is narrow and, I think, legitimate: refuse the counterfeit. A Christ who is only like God cannot save. A control that is only like a control cannot hold.

The painting is of a room that argued. That is the right memory. Not marble as a brand color. Argument, until the words were true.

**Contra mundum. Code obscurum. Christ supreme.**
