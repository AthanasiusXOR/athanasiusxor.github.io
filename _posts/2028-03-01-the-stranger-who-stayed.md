A grant is a form of hospitality. The keeper opens a door for a stranger, for a time, for a named work. Scripture honors that opening. The plate at the top is the failure that comes after the honor. The door is still open. The lamp is still burning. The guest was supposed to have gone home.

> "Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares."
> ~ **Hebrews 13:2**

## The door opened on purpose

The assessment needs a way to touch the house, or it is a rumor about a house it never entered. An account is made. A path is allowed. A vendor is given a key. A volunteer is trusted with a shared way in. These are not sins. They are hospitality with edges. Hebrews does not blush at the stranger. Abraham did not. The edge is the part the verse does not cancel. The stranger is received. He is not made a son by the reception, and he is not given the run of the tents after the visit.

The offensive team is the clearest case, because the grant has a date written on it. The way in exists so the emulation can press the house and then stop. When the report is delivered, that way is supposed to die. An account still valid, a path still open, a foothold the team can still use, is the guest's bag still in the room. The keeper who says to leave it, just in case the testers are needed, has converted a visit into a residency. The testers who leave it without saying so have moved in without asking.

## Who else stays

The same lamp burns for people who were not on the assessment. The vendor whose contract ended and whose access did not. The volunteer who changed churches and whose login did not. The employee who was the dynasty of one head and whose ways in were never written down, so they were never ended. Hospitality becomes an entrance with no grant the moment the reason for the welcome is gone and the welcome remains. November's essay said the charge must be receivable by the next steward. This one says the previous stranger must actually leave.

Angels unawares is not a standing key. The verse is a reason to open the door with love. It is not a reason to stop knowing who is in the house. The elders who cannot say who can still read the names have already failed a question March asked. The stranger who stayed is often the reason they cannot answer it.

## End the visit

What the keeper should be able to see is a list of welcomes and a date beside each one. Who was let in. Why. When the why ended. Whether the way in ended with it. The offensive team owes the keeper that list for everything the engagement opened, and owes the ending of those openings as part of the work, not as a courtesy if someone remembers.

How a way in is kept alive does not belong on this field-post. The judgment does. Hospitality has a morning after. The lamp in the plate is beautiful, and it is late. Put it out. Close the door. The stranger who was received in love is honored by being allowed to leave.

**Contra mundum. Code obscurum. Christ supreme.**
