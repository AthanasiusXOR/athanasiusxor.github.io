> "Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah, not like the covenant that I made with their fathers."
> ~ **Jeremiah 31:31–32**

I read the covenants as progressive and as actually new. One saving purpose of God, unfolded through covenants that are not identical, brought to fullness in the new covenant in Christ. That covenant is new in its substance. It is not a second administration of an unchanged bond, with Moses left in place beside Christ under a new style of management. Jeremiah says "not like." Hebrews 8 says the new covenant has made the first one obsolete. Luke 22 calls the cup the new covenant in Christ's blood. I will not spend those sentences as a metaphor for a statement of work. I will argue them as themselves, and then I will bring back only the smaller true thing, which the Epistle already named. When the work is over, you leave, and the keeper remains the keeper.

The nostalgia this craft will not confess is the wish that the bond not end. A tester needs the client to stay weak so that he can stay necessary. An engagement is renewed because absence feels like negligence. A key is kept "just in case." The theology of the covenants judges that nostalgia, not by turning a network into redemptive history, but by training a Christian to recognize a bond that has no end as a bond that has begun to play at being the covenant of grace. That covenant does not end, because the mediator does not die again. Your contract is not that covenant. It ends.

### What I am not doing with Jeremiah

I am not mapping the Abrahamic promise onto a master service agreement, or Sinai onto a policy binder, or the new covenant onto a mature security program. That game can be played by a clever man for an hour, and it teaches him to be clever. It does not teach him the covenants. The covenants are God's, sworn in history, fulfilled in the Son. Progressive covenantalism, as I hold it, cares about that progress: each covenant unfolds the kingdom, later covenants carry earlier ones to a greater fullness, and the new covenant is the eschatological covenant in Christ's blood, not an updated administration of Moses. The 1689 Confession speaks of one covenant of grace revealed progressively. I gladly confess one saving purpose and one mediator. I also confess, with Jeremiah, that the new covenant is not like the covenant made with the fathers. The argument between those emphases is a real argument among Baptists who love the same confession. This essay will not settle it in a footnote. It will not raid it for an org chart.

What the doctrine does to a man is simpler than the raid. He learns that God brings bonds to their fullness and does not leave his people under a tutor once the Son has come. He learns that an everlasting covenant is everlasting because of who swore it. He learns to be suspicious of human arrangements that cannot imagine their own end. Suspicion is not cynicism. A marriage is not suspect because it is for life. A marriage is a covenant of its own kind, and God named it. An engagement to try a house is not a marriage, not a church, and not the new covenant. It is a temporary authority, borrowed, written, and mortal. When it cannot die, it has misunderstood what it was.

### The human instrument of the end

The end has to be written while the beginning is being written. Otherwise the nostalgia writes it later, in its own favor.

The public documents know this in their own dialect. [NIST's guide to information security testing](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/115/final) treats rules of engagement as part of the assessment itself: what may be touched, what must stop, when the work is over. The [NCSC's account of penetration testing](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/penetration-testing) tells the buyer to settle scope before the guest arrives. These are circumstances. They are the right circumstances. A bond with no edge is not a more serious bond. It is a bond that has already begun to replace the keeper. I am not reproducing their procedures. I am agreeing with their modesty. A test is a dated thing.

Then the access has to be able to die in fact, not only on the last page. Accounts end. Paths end. The copies end. "Just in case" is the phrase by which a guest becomes a second administration. The Epistle said the smaller true thing, and I will say it again because the covenants are why a Christian should recognize it. Resembling an adversary and becoming one are different acts. Becoming one is wanting what he wants: the trophy, the permanence, the secret kept from the head of the house. The new covenant does not leave a rival administration standing beside the Son. Your leftover key is not Moses. It is smaller than that, and the smallness is not an excuse. It is a key you were finished with.

[Geneva](https://github.com/AthanasiusXOR/geneva-ludus) is the range I will show, because a public lab can be ruled and reduced without holding a neighbor's house open. Even there the bond with a particular experiment ends. A range that cannot be torn down has begun to rule the gardener. A client's house that cannot be left has begun to rule the guest. Dominion, if it is vice-regency and not a seizure, includes the right and the duty to stop.

### The nostalgia, named

The feeling is easy to baptize. You know the house. A new firm would have to learn it. The findings are only half remediated. Love, you say, remains. Sometimes love does remain, as availability, under a new and written charge, with a new end already dated. Often the feeling is not love. It is the fear of being unnecessary, dressed as fidelity. The covenants will not let that dress pass. God does not keep his people weak so that a tutor can keep his post. A keeper who is never allowed to become competent without you has been made a minor in his own house. That is not care. It is a living.

There is a counterpart for the keeper. He can prefer a guest who never closes, because a permanent guest feels like safety and asks less courage than learning his own boundary. The covenant-shaped question for him is whether he is hiring a witness or adopting a regent. A witness comes, tells the truth, and goes. A regent stays and slowly becomes the reason the house cannot speak for itself. You may hire the first again. You should be afraid of the second, even when he quotes Scripture, and especially when he quotes it to explain why he still has the key.

The cup our Lord gave is the new covenant in his blood. It does not expire, because he does not. Your badge does. Write the end. Keep the end. When the morning comes, leave the house to the keeper who was there before you and who will answer for it after you. The bond was real. It is over.
