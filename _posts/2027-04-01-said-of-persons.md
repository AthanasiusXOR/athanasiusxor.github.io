> "Everyone who makes a practice of sinning also practices lawlessness; sin is lawlessness."
> ~ **1 John 3:4**

The craft has a loose mouth. It says a system sinned. It says a control failed morally. It says the code is guilty and the finding is a judgment. Then, in the next breath, it says the operator is a neutral instrument and the only moral question left is whether the report was formatted. Both sentences cannot stand. Sin is said of persons. A bug is not a transgressor. The confession is able to tell these apart, and the work gets worse when it will not.

The Second London Confession treats sin as the transgression of the law of God. Our first parents fell by it. Their posterity comes into the world in sin. From that corruption proceed actual transgressions. The chapter is about men and women. It is not about compilers. Finitude is the condition of a creature: limited sight, limited strength, a made thing that can break. Malice, negligence, and pride are the condition of a fallen will. A cracked vessel is not a wicked vessel. The hands that ship what they will not look at, or that look and laugh, are a different subject. We tell the truth about risk without preaching at a compiler. We preach, including at ourselves, where persons have been greedy, slothful, or cruel.

### A name for a weakness is not a name for a soul

The public vocabulary is better than our jokes, if we let it stay in its lane.

[CVE](https://www.cve.org/) gives a public identifier to a vulnerability, so that a weakness in a product can be talked about without each house inventing a private name for the same crack. [CWE](https://cwe.mitre.org/) names classes of weakness, the kinds of mistake a made thing can embody. Neither identifier is a guilty verdict against a person. A number can be true. It does not accuse. The accusation begins when a person knew, or refused to know, or used the crack against a neighbor, or hid it in order to keep a trophy. That is lawlessness. The jar did not practice it.

This is why the way a weakness is told matters as much as the weakness. The [CERT Guide to Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/) is a public argument for telling a weakness to the people who can act, in an order that does not turn the telling into a spectacle. [RFC 9116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html), the small file called security.txt, is one circumstance of that duty: a house can say, in a place a finder can read, where a report of a weakness should go. I am not laying out a disclosure procedure. I am marking the moral shape. A weakness is reported so a keeper can act. It is not preached at the software, and it is not spent as a story that feeds the finder.

There is no person in this work who is righteous by the work. The finding can be true while the finder is proud. Pride does not falsify the CVE. It does damn the pose. The pose has forms the guild rewards: the sigh, the joke at the defender's expense, the pleasure of having been the one who saw, the severity nudged upward because fear buys budget. Those are moral events. They are said of the person. A perfectly accurate report can carry them, the way a true sentence can be spoken in order to crush. The truth of the sentence is not the righteousness of the speaker.

### Finitude, negligence, malice

Keep the three apart or the debrief becomes a sermon against the wrong defendant.

Finitude: a person did not know, and could not reasonably have known, and the made thing broke in a way creatures' things break. Say that. Do not call it sin to make the report feel serious. Seriousness that lies about the soul is not severity. It is false witness. The keeper needs the size of the crack. He does not need you to invent a transgressor so the room will stay awake.

Negligence: a person could have looked, and the charge of his office was to look, and he would not. That is a moral fact. It is not identical to an exploit existing. Sloth in a keeper is said of the keeper. It is also said of the tester who will not check his own report, who ships a guess as a sighting, who leaves the access in place because retrieval is tedious. Negligence is not the jar. It is the watchman.

Malice: a person meant the harm, or meant the trophy, or meant the neighbor's exposure. This can be the thief the engagement resembles. It can also be the tester who keeps a copy, who widens a scope in secret, who tells the weakness as entertainment. Malice is lawlessness with a will behind it. The confession does not let a will hide inside a passive verb. "The environment was compromised" is sometimes a true description of a system. It is a lie if it is used to smuggle out the person who compromised it, or the person who left it open on purpose.

Original sin does not flatten these. Every person in the room is a sinner, the careful keeper included, the precise tester included. That levels boasting. It does not level findings. "All have sinned" is not a reason to shrug at cruelty, and it is not a reason to call a null pointer a transgression. The gospel is for persons. There is no atonement for a library, and a library does not need one. Persons do. The man who must make the software guilty in order to feel righteous has not yet heard that his own pride is the thing with a soul.

### What the report is allowed to say

It may say what broke, how sure the sighting is, and what the keeper can do. It may say, when it is true and when it serves the keeper, that a person in an office neglected a duty. It may not enjoy saying it. It may not preach at a compiler to avoid preaching at itself. The strongest sentence in a bad report is often the one that calls a configuration wicked and leaves the author's own method unexamined. The Epistle already said this in short. The finding can be true while the finder is proud.

Say the crack. Do not baptize it. Do not damn the jar. Damn, in the proper forum and with the proper fear, the will that used it, including yours when yours is the will. God already knows the difference. The report should not be the place we pretend we do not.
