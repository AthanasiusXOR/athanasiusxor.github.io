January asked who is giving the work. February asks a prior question. What was built?

People hand you a drawing. Boxes, lines, a note that this path is private and that door is shut. The drawing is a promise. It says what the estate was meant to be. The tenant is what the account actually contains: the identities, the paths, the doors, the things that answer when the house is reached from outside. Promised is not made.

The plate at the top holds the two apart. The gold drawing is the word. The racks, with a door the drawing did not have, are the act.

> "And likewise the cup after they had eaten, saying, 'This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.'"
> ~ **Luke 22:20**

## The two words stay different

The October essay already refused to collapse these words in Scripture. Grace is promised from the garden on. Genesis 3:15 is a promise. Abraham is a promise. David is a promise. What is not yet made, in those centuries, is the new covenant Jeremiah describes. The cup at the table is the making. Christ pours out the covenant in his blood, and Hebrews calls that covenant enacted. A promise kept by being redescribed, until the pictures counted as the supper, would not be the supper.

![A seed still glowing, labeled Promised, and a cup labeled Made.](/assets/img/site/promise-and-cup.jpg)

The technical error is the smaller echo, and it is still an error. A drawing of the estate is a real document. It names an intent, an owner, and a picture of what should be closed. The tenant is the other half of the question. It is the thing that was made. A review that blesses the diagram and never asks what the account contains has treated a promise as a finished act. The drawing was real. It was the word. The house is the other thing.

Drift is the ordinary shape of the gap. Someone changed a path after the review. A door the drawing shut was opened for a weekend and left open. The picture says private, and the private thing answers from the road. The first hour of that gap does not require a villain. It requires two documents kept apart until they agree. A template is a promise of an estate. It becomes an estate when the tenant exists and matches it.

## What the owner should be able to see

The public claim stops at recognition.

The owner should be able to lay the promise next to the making. What the drawing says is shut. What answers. Which identities exist. Which paths are reachable. Where the picture and the tenant disagree, the disagreement is the finding. A control that lives on the slide and not in the account has not been made, and it must not be reported as though it had.

The comparison is the question. A thicker diagram is a louder promise. You can draw a perfect house and be standing in a different one. The record of what is deployed, read against the record of what was promised, is where a steward looks. The look belongs to the owner.

How to walk someone else's account, how to turn a picture of an estate into a way in, and how to build the paths a drawing conceals do not belong on this field-post. They belong, when they belong at all, inside an authorized engagement with a scope and a way to undo the damage. The judgment belongs here. A control that trusts the promise and never inspects the making will pass an estate that was never built.

## The repair is agreement

One repair changes the tenant until it keeps the promise. The other amends the promise until it tells the truth about the tenant. Construction is allowed. So is confession. Using the drawing as a substitute for the visit is the failure this essay is for.

What arrives in Christ stays at a distance that should remain respectful. The promises were kept by being made, in his blood. An estate does not inherit a smaller version of that glory. It does inherit the duty. Say what you meant. Go and see what you made. Keep the first sentence from standing in for the second.

**Contra mundum. Code obscurum. Christ supreme.**
