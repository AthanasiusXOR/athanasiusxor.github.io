Saturday, October 31, is the five hundred and ninth anniversary of the day Martin Luther made his protest against indulgences public. The hammer on the castle-church door is a better story than the surviving paperwork, and the paperwork is enough. A professor disputed a trade in merit. The dispute did not stay on the door.

Reformed Baptists will mark the day. Some of us on the Saturday itself. The churches will bring it into the assembly on the Lord's Day, November 1. We are not borrowing a Lutheran feast and hoping no one looks at the font.

> "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek. For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith for faith, as it is written, 'The righteous shall live by faith.'"
> ~ **Romans 1:16–17**

## What we are keeping

We are keeping a recovery, not a costume.

The indulgence trade had taught people to trust a treasury of merit they could buy a share in. Luther, driven back to Romans, found that the righteousness of God is not a standard the sinner funds. It is a gift, given to the ungodly through faith in Christ. That sentence is the center. A great deal of later commentary grew up around it, and we do not sign all of the commentary.

Five words are enough to name what we received.

1. **Scripture alone.** The norm of faith and life is the written Word. Not a magisterium. Not a private revelation that outranks the text.
2. **Grace alone.** Salvation is not a wage.
3. **Faith alone.** Faith is the empty hand. It is not a work smuggled in under a longer name.
4. **Christ alone.** The mediator is not a ladder of saints, priests, and partial merits.
5. **To God alone the glory.** The recovery was for worship, or it was a quarrel among scholars.

The Second London Confession, which Reformed Baptists still hold, begins on Scripture, confesses justification by faith apart from works, and gives the ordinances to the church that faith creates. That is the Reformation we are keeping. October 31 is a witness to it. It is not the source of it. The source is the gospel the apostles already preached.

## What we are not keeping

I can love the rose in the window and refuse to pretend it is our seal.

Luther was not a Baptist. He held infants inside the covenant by baptism, and he confessed the supper in a way we do not. Calvin, and the Westminster divines after him, are worth reading with gratitude, and gratitude is not a merger. On the ordinances the Reformed Baptist dissents twice: once from Rome, and once from the paedobaptist majority of the Reformation churches.

That second dissent is not a hobby added to an otherwise Presbyterian church. Believers' baptism is the outward sign kept for those who profess the faith the Reformation preached. We do not repair justification and then fog the church. A righteousness that is really a gift, paired with a church that cannot say who has received it, is an unfinished recovery.

We also do not need Luther to have been our pastor. He was a man, and a man under the doctrine he was compelled to say again. We receive the doctrine. We test the man by it. Heroes are a bad liturgy. The rose can stay in the window as a witness. It does not go on our pulpit.

![Luther's rose in stained glass: a cross set in a heart](/assets/img/site/luther-rose.jpg)

## How a church should spend the day

Not with a costume, and not with a lecture that never reaches a prayer.

Read Romans 1, or Romans 3, or Galatians 2. Pray. Sing. Preach justification as if someone in the room is still trying to pay God back. If the thirty-first is a Saturday and the church gathers on the first of November, carry the memory into that gathering. The date is a witness. The assembly is the point.

The rest of this field-post will go back to ramparts and reports. This is the prior claim. A control that only resembles the real thing will fail. A righteousness that only resembles a gift, and is in fact a wage, will fail too. October 31 is the church refusing the counterfeit at the center.

**Contra mundum. Code obscurum. Christ supreme.**
