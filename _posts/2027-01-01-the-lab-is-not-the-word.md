> "You have given him dominion over the works of your hands; you have put all things under his feet."
> ~ **Psalm 8:6**
>
> "The law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul; the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple."
> ~ **Psalm 19:7**

The psalms belong together. One gives the creature a crown and a charge. The other gives him a law that revives the soul. The crown is real. The law is perfect. A man who treats his dominion as the truth has forgotten which speech makes wise the simple. A man who treats the law as a substitute for the work has forgotten that God put the works of his hands under human feet.

A testing network sits between those two speeches. It is a made thing: machines, names, routes, and rules, assembled so that an authorized tester can try a house and tell the keeper what the trial showed. We build it because we are vice-regents. We were made in the image of God and told to subdue a world and keep it. A lab is a small world of that kind. We have dominion over it. Inside it we test. The testing can peel back the craft itself, the concealment we call OPSEC and the methods we call TTPs, until the engineer can see what he has been doing. That seeing is a gift of the dominion. It is not the truth that comes from Scripture.

This essay is about that difference. The doctrine underneath it is still revelation. General revelation, special revelation, and the sufficiency of Scripture. The infrastructure, if it is fit to exist under that doctrine, is succinct, authoritative, and fallible. I am not going to build the range on this page. The laboratory is [Geneva](https://github.com/AthanasiusXOR/geneva-ludus), a Ludus range on a host named Wittenberg. The diagram is not the doctrine. The doctrine will not be smuggled in as a diagram.

### I. Vice-regents, not the Maker

"Then God said, 'Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And let them have dominion'" (Genesis 1:26). The next verses do not leave the grant in the abstract. God makes them male and female in his image, blesses them, and says, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it, and have dominion" (Genesis 1:28). Psalm 8 looks at that same creature, crowned with glory and honor, and says the dominion is over the works of God's hands. Psalm 115 draws the boundary in one line. "The heavens are the LORD's heavens, but the earth he has given to the children of man" (Psalm 115:16).

This is vice-regency. A regent rules in the name of the king. He does not become the king because the realm answers to his voice. We make things, and in that making we image the Maker. The likeness is real and it is analogical. God creates by his word from nothing, and the word he speaks in revelation is true because he speaks it. We arrange what he has already made. We name, separate, cultivate, and keep. Genesis 2 puts the man in a garden "to work it and keep it." Dominion is labor and custody. It is not aseity.

A lab is a garden of that second kind. We design a world small enough to try. We give it boundaries. We put things in it that will answer when they are touched, including things that fail, because a garden that cannot fail cannot teach a keeper anything about failure. Then we rule it. The rule is not a mood. It is the right and the duty to say what the instrument is for, what it may touch, when it stops, and what must be torn out when it has outlived the question. A range no one will reduce is not a dominion. It is a thicket that has begun to rule the gardener.

The likeness to the Lord has a hard edge, and the edge is the point of this essay. We create as images. We do not create as God. Our making does not carry his authority, his infallibility, or his sufficiency. When a red-team engineer says, in the pride of a finished range, that he has made a world and therefore he knows, he has slid from vice-regent to counterfeit maker. The lab can be excellent and still be a creature's work. The heavens are still the Lord's.

Persons are not inside the grant the way machines are. The fish of the sea and the works of his hands are put under our feet. The neighbor is not. The names, the money, the records, and the prayers that pass through a test are not the soil of the garden. Dominion over a lab is dominion over the instrument. It is not dominion over the people the instrument can see. A vice-regent who forgets that has confused Genesis 1 with a license, and the license was never written.

### II. What the dominion is for

Inside that grant, the lab has a particular work for people who test houses. It lets us see our own craft.

OPSEC, in this vocation, is the set of habits by which an operator remains hard to see. A TTP is a way of working that can be named: a pattern of action the craft repeats until it feels like nature. Both of them hide. OPSEC hides the operator. A TTP, once it is skilled, hides inside competence. The hands know it so well that the conscience stops visiting it. A range is one of the places the visit can happen. We build a world we rule, and then we run our own methods in it until the methods are visible. What was concealed in the name of realism becomes something that can be told. What was only a habit becomes something that can be judged.

That is a revelation in the old, modest sense of the word. Something covered is uncovered. The engineer provides it, and the engineer receives it. He peels back his own OPSEC and his own TTPs, not so that the world can have a manual, and not so that he can admire the peel, but so that he can see the thing he has been doing and bring it under a rule he did not write. Defenders receive a cousin of the same gift when a finding is returned to them: a boundary they could not see is made speakable. In both directions the lab is a light on a human work. It is not the light that God is, and it is not the Word God spoke.

I want that sentence to stay sharp, because the analogy is genuinely useful and genuinely dangerous. It is useful because vice-regents are supposed to know their realm. A keeper who will not look at his own methods is not ruling them. A tester who will not let his concealment be examined has made a garden he refuses to walk. It is dangerous because "revelation" is already a doctrinal name. If the uncovering the lab performs is called revelation without a distinction, the engineer will start to hear his own findings with the fear and the trust that belong to Scripture. He will think the peel is the truth. It is a witness to the craft. The truth that judges the craft comes from somewhere else.

So the lab, under this doctrine, is succinct, authoritative, and fallible. The three words are how a vice-regent rules a testing world without pretending to be the Lord of it.

**Succinct** means the build says only what the charge requires it to say. Every added part is added speech. Dominion is not the right to an infinite estate. The earth was given, not the heavens. A succinct range is a range that has accepted the size of the grant. The operator asked a bounded question and built the smallest honest instrument that can pursue it, including the question he must ask of his own methods. What does not serve the question does not get to live in the lab because it was interesting, or because another team has it, or because a diagram looked mature with more boxes.

**Authoritative** means the build has a warrant, and it speaks only inside that warrant. Dominion in Genesis is delegated. Someone with the right to bind the house authorized the trial, or the builder authorized a laboratory whose only inhabitants are his own instruments. The range acts in that name for a named time, against a named boundary, and then stops. Its report is authoritative in a small and borrowed sense: these instruments, used this way, returned this, and these methods, once uncovered, look like this. It is not authoritative because the operator is clever. Authority here can be shown, limited, and revoked. A range that cannot be turned off has left vice-regency behind. It has begun to govern its maker.

**Fallible** means the build is a creature's instrument, and it is designed as one. Sensors miss. Logs lie by omission. A quiet hour is not a safe hour. An uncovered method can be misread. A finding is a witness with a vantage, not a vision. The image of God is a true image and a creaturely one. Vice-regents err. The lab records what was not seen. It distinguishes what was returned from what was inferred. It is built so that a competent reader can doubt it without first having to defeat a theater of completeness.

These three stand together. A succinct lab is easier to rule, because the gardener can see the beds. An authoritative lab is easier to bound, because the warrant is written and the lifetime is real. A fallible lab is the only kind that will not impersonate the law of the Lord. Take one away and the other two sour. A succinct system with no warrant is a small theft. An authoritative system that will not confess its misses is a small tyranny. A fallible system with no discipline of size becomes a fog in which no one can tell a miss from a method, or a habit from a sin.

### III. What the words are not

They are not a shopping list, and they are not a comparison of providers. Feasibility, cost, and scale belong to prudence, and prudence is part of keeping a garden. A charge that a congregation can bear is not the charge a global enterprise can bear. No arrangement of rented machines turns a lab into revelation, and none of them turns the builder into the Lord. The moral shape is the same in a spare room and in a large estate: succinct, under a warrant, and honest about what it cannot see.

They are also not a permission to publish a method. This page will not print the habits of concealment or the patterns of action. Geneva is the other publication. It can hold a range for people who already have a charge. This essay will not be turned into its manual, and the repository will not be allowed to launder a procedure by standing next to a verse. The peeling this essay talks about is a moral act: the craft is made visible to the people who must judge it. The peeling is not a lesson in how to disappear, and it is not a lesson in how to imitate a thief.

And they are not a claim that infrastructure is holy, or that a clean design is a sanctified design. God is holy. The lab is wood and wire, or their rented equivalents. It can be faithful to a charge. It cannot be set apart as God is set apart. Ruling it well is obedience. Worshiping it is a confusion of the image with the Maker.

### IV. Two ways God has spoken

The Second London Confession, in its first paragraph, calls Holy Scripture the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience. In the same breath it says that the light of nature, and the works of creation and providence, manifest the goodness, wisdom, and power of God so far as to leave men inexcusable, and that they are not sufficient to give the knowledge of God and of his will which is necessary for salvation. There is a true speech of God in the things that are made. There is a sufficient speech of God in Scripture. A third speech, the speech of our instruments, is not on that list.

The Belgic Confession says we know God by two means. The first is the creation, preservation, and government of the universe. The second is his holy and divine Word, by which he makes himself known more openly. Our labs are not a third means of knowing God. They are a means of knowing a realm we were told to keep. The uncovering they perform can be true of that realm and still be silent about Christ.

General revelation is the first speech. "For what can be known about God is plain to them, because God has shown it to them. For his invisible attributes, namely, his eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly perceived, ever since the creation of the world, in the things that have been made. So they are without excuse" (Romans 1:19–20). It is plain. It is God's own showing. It leaves men without excuse. It does not save. Psalm 19 begins with the heavens declaring the glory of God and does not stay there. The speech of the sky does not revive the soul. The law does that.

Special revelation is God addressing his people by words. "Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son" (Hebrews 1:1–2). That speech has been committed to writing. We are not waiting for a further installment, from a prophet or from a range, to know the things necessary for God's glory, man's salvation, faith, and life. The canon is the rule. The sky is not the rule. A dashboard is not the rule. A peeled-back method is not the rule.

I am going to bring this to the lab and mark where the likeness stops. The last error this craft needs is to hear its own uncovering in the accent of Hebrews 1.

### V. The uncovering is not his speech

There is a true likeness, and it is structural. God discloses. He does not leave his works in a cipher he refuses to open. A vice-regent, imaging that honesty inside a small dominion, ought not leave his own craft in a cipher he refuses to open. The lab peels OPSEC and TTPs back so they can be told. Concealment that cannot be confessed to the keeper, or to the operator's own conscience under the Word, has stopped being a circumstance of the test and has become a hiding. Methods that cannot be named cannot be repented of, and they cannot be justified either. Dominion includes the duty to walk the garden and say what is growing there.

The false likeness is the one I have to refuse by name. The network is not the heavens. The lab's unveiling is not general revelation of the eternal power and divine nature of God, and it is not special revelation of his will. Romans 1 will not support a theology of the stack. Hebrews 1 will not support a theology of the finding. What the instruments return is a disclosure of human work: machines persons configured, habits persons practiced, methods persons chose. Call it a witness. Call it, if you must, a creaturely uncovering. Do not call it the truth that comes from Scripture, or you will fear it and trust it in the wrong way.

General revelation is plain, and men suppress it. A lab culture that treats difficulty of reading as evidence of depth has learned the opposite lesson. Hard to see is sometimes what a charge requires of a method while the trial runs. It is not what makes a finding more like the knowledge of God. God's speech in creation is public enough to leave the world without excuse. A method that has been peeled back and still cannot be explained to the keeper who must judge it has not yet become a witness. The uncovering exists to be told. Suppression, in this work, is the operator who loves his OPSEC too much to let it be seen, and the keeper who prefers a green badge to a true sentence.

And the uncovering does not save. A house that has been shown an open door is accountable for the door. A craftsman who has been shown his own method is accountable for the method. Neither showing is the gospel. The law of the Lord makes wise the simple. A report does not. If the hour in the range is the most theological hour of the month, the hour has been asked to do what only the Word can do.

### VI. The voice the lab must not borrow

Special revelation is God speaking, and in these last days speaking by the Son. Its authority is the authority of the speaker. The lab has no such speaker. Its authority is the delegated authority of a vice-regent: temporary, methodological, and open to appeal. The sentence it is allowed to say can be audited. Under this mandate, with these means, in this window, this was returned, and this was not. This method, once uncovered, does this. That is a true sentence about a craft. It is not "thus says the Lord."

The theft of voice is the characteristic sin of a mature-looking range. The panes multiply. The names sound official. The report ceases to say "we saw" and begins to say "it is." That shift is a doctrinal event. "It is," without a method and a limit, is the accent of a word that claims to bind the conscience. Scripture may speak that way because God has spoken. A lab speaks that way only by imitation. The imitation trains the keeper to receive a fallible witness as a rule of faith and life, and it trains the operator to enjoy being believed at that pitch. A vice-regent who wants that pitch wants the crown without the king.

Nothing is to be added to the whole counsel of God, whether by new revelations of the Spirit or by traditions of men. A finding is not a new revelation of the Spirit. The Spirit does not beacon. Technical discovery, and the uncovering of our own methods, belong to the light of nature and to trained attention inside a dominion. They are real goods. The moment they are preached as God now saying a new thing about the house, or about the operator's authority to remain, an addition has been made. The canon is not open to the latest result. The latest result stands under the canon and is judged by it.

This is the order the work actually needs. The lab can peel a method back. It cannot tell the operator whether he may use it, whether he may stay, whether he may tell the story, whether the neighbor in the data is material. Those are questions of faith and life. Scripture speaks to them. The instruments do not. An operator who knows the route and does not know the prohibition is not suffering from a gap in his range. He is suppressing a word he was given from outside the garden.

There is comfort in this, and the comfort is not soft. Because the lab is not the Word, a missed finding is not a failed inspiration, and a severe finding is not a prophetic burden. The man in Christ is not justified by the completeness of his infrastructure and not condemned by its gaps. He owes the keeper an honest account of a fallible instrument, and he owes God an account of the methods the instrument uncovered. He does not owe the room an oracle. That freedom is what makes succinctness possible. A man who needs the lab to speak with the accent of Scripture will keep adding to it until the accent arrives. It will not arrive. He will only get a louder instrument, and a quieter conscience.

### VII. Sufficiency, and the circumstances of dominion

Sufficiency is the confession's defense of Scripture against both anxiety and arrogance. The Second London Confession, at 1.6, teaches that the whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man's salvation, faith, and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture or by good and necessary consequence drawn from it, and that nothing is to be added. It then says that some circumstances of human action, common to societies, are to be ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word, which are always to be observed.

A testing network is a circumstance of dominion. How it is housed, how large it is, what it is allowed to touch, how long it lives, how a method is made visible inside it: these are questions of prudence. Whether a man may enter a house at all, whether he may lie to the keeper, whether he may keep the access, whether the persons in the data may be used as material, whether a method the lab has uncovered is lawful: these are questions of faith and life. Scripture is sufficient for the second list. It does not pretend to be a plan for the first. The operator must not pretend that his answers to the first have become sufficient for the second.

This is where the three words receive the doctrine of the image.

Scripture is sufficient. The lab must be succinct. We do not build a second source of moral knowledge because we are anxious that the Word left the craft out. The Word did not leave the man out, and the man is the one who holds the tools. Succinctness means we also do not build a second source of technical theater because we are anxious that a bounded instrument looks too small for a vice-regent. The grant was never a promise of an infinite garden. A baroque lab is often a man's attempt to manufacture sufficiency, or to manufacture a majesty he was not given. He wants enough panes that the conclusion no longer needs a mandate, a method, or a doubt, and enough estate that his dominion feels like the Lord's. That feeling is counterfeit. The law of the Lord is perfect. The wall of panes is not.

Scripture is certain because of who spoke. The lab must be authoritative only with a borrowed and inspectable warrant. Certainty is not a goal for infrastructure. Warrant is. A keeper, and the builder who claims to rule the range, should be able to say who authorized the trial, what it was allowed to do, when it stops, and what kind of sentence the output is. If those questions cannot be answered, the build has power and does not have authority. Power without warrant is the thing a vice-regent is forbidden to want. The heavens were not given to him.

Scripture is infallible. The lab must be fallible on purpose, and so must the man who reads what it peels back. Infallible is a word for the Spirit's authorship of the text. Two sensors that agree can both be wrong. An uncovered method can be named badly. Agreement feels like canon. It is not. Fallibility, confessed in the architecture, sounds like this: the report states the vantage, the negative result, and the inference, and it refuses to let the inference wear the vantage's clothing. The method it has made visible is offered for judgment. It is not offered as the judgment. Design the lab so that the pretending is difficult. A succinct system helps. There is less theater to hide in, and less room for a regent to mistake himself for the king.

Christian prudence is the work of choosing those circumstances under the general rules of the Word. It asks what the charge actually is. It asks what the house can sustain. It asks what will be true on the day after the engagement, when the instrument should be gone and the keeper should still be the keeper. It asks whether the build's size serves the neighbor or the operator's delight in being the man with the estate. It asks, once OPSEC and the TTPs have been peeled back, whether the man will actually let the Word speak about what he now sees. Prudence can be wrong, because it is a creaturely judgment. That is why it stays under the Word and does not become a new revelation. A lab that was sized badly can be repented of and reduced. A lab that has been treated as the truth cannot be reduced without a crisis of faith. Build the kind of thing a Christian can shrink without feeling that God has gone silent.

### VIII. What follows for the work

A testing network fit for a vice-regent has a few marks. They are marks of judgment, not a procedure.

It has a charge, and the charge is smaller than the operator's curiosity. He rules the range. The range does not rule him. It lives only as long as the charge lives. It touches only what the charge names. When the charge ends, the instrument ends, including the quiet parts he was proud of keeping. A succinct lab that outlives its mandate has become a second administration with good design taste. The gardener has lost the garden.

It makes the craft speakable. The OPSEC and the TTPs that the trial uses are not a private priesthood. They are uncovered far enough that the keeper, and the operator under Scripture, can say what was done. The account of the trial is plain. Obfuscation of a method, while the trial runs and the charge requires it, is a circumstance. Obfuscation of the finding, and a permanent refusal to let one's own methods be judged, is suppression. Dominion that will not give an account is not the image of the God who speaks.

It speaks in the grammar of a witness. "Returned." "Not returned." "Inferred." "Unknown." "This is the method, uncovered." It does not speak in the grammar of Hebrews 1. The Son has spoken. The lab has measured, and it has peeled. The report that wants to be believed at the pitch of Scripture is the report that most needs an editor.

It is sized to the question and to the house. A congregation does not need the estate that flatters an operator. An enterprise does not get a moral pass to build a fog because it can afford one. Scale changes prudence. It does not change who the Maker is. Sufficiency does not grow when the budget grows, and the grant of dominion does not grow into the heavens.

It expects to be wrong, and it makes the wrongness visible. What was not seen is part of the truth owed to the keeper. A report with no unknown in it has often promoted inference into sight. Fallibility, hidden, becomes a lie. Fallibility, stated, becomes a service a vice-regent can actually use, because he is not offended that his instrument is a creature.

And it stays on the creature's side of the line the psalms draw. God gave dominion over the works of his hands. He did not give his infallible speech into our keeping as something we could rewrite in a range. The operator who holds both sentences can build a small, warranted, doubtful instrument, rule it, let it show him his own craft, and then go home. He will not ask the instrument to revive anyone's soul. He will not ask the peeled-back method to be the law. He already has the law. It is perfect. It was not written by his build.

### The names on the range

The range is called Geneva. The host is called Wittenberg. The names are a confession before they are a label, and they are easy to spend badly.

Wittenberg is where the questions were nailed to a door in public. Geneva is where a church tried to order its life by the Word it had been given. Neither city was a machine, and neither city was the Lord. Naming a range after them does not make the range a church, and it does not make the range's output Scripture. It does say what the builder claims to be doing: a public question, under a known rule, in a made thing he intends to rule rather than worship.

[Geneva](https://github.com/AthanasiusXOR/geneva-ludus) is a Ludus range. It pairs a deliberately vulnerable directory lab, of the kind used for authorized practice, with a testing network kept apart from those targets. The separation is the point. The instrument is not the house. Even when the house was built to be tried, the instrument does not become the rule of the man who tries it. The range is a dominion. It can be ruled well or abandoned to its own overgrowth. It can uncover the methods that run in it, or it can hide them under the name of realism. It can sit under a warrant, or it can merely be powered on. The repository does not settle those questions by existing. Scripture does, and the man who claims the dominion has to let it.

The build stays there. It cannot hold a second canon, and this essay will not be turned into the missing page of its manual. If the range cannot be described as succinct, authoritative, and fallible, it is not yet fit to be called a Christian instrument, however elegantly it runs. If the man who runs it cannot say where Scripture has already spoken about the methods the range peeled back, the elegance is beside the point. He is a vice-regent. He is not the Lord. The Word was sufficient before the first machine was rented. It remains the truth when the last one is destroyed.
