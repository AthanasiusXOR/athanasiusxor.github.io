People ask which system a Reformed Baptist is using. They expect Westminster, or they expect the chart that runs from Darby to a restored nation. I am using a third reading. The name for it is progressive covenantalism. The name is a label. The claim is older than the label. God has one plan. He made it known through covenants that keep their own names. Those covenants arrive in Christ, and the new covenant is actually new.

> "For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the LORD: I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts. And I will be their God, and they shall be my people. And no longer shall each one teach his neighbor and each his brother, saying, 'Know the LORD,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest, declares the LORD. For I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more."
> ~ **Jeremiah 31:33–34**

## The covenants stay themselves

Progressive does not mean the church is a phase we will outgrow. It means the Bible moves, and the movement is the point. Adam, Noah, Abraham, Israel at Sinai, David, and then the new covenant in the blood of Christ. Each covenant is a real act of God. None of them is a costume worn by one contract that was secretly the same arrangement the whole time. None of them is a side program that keeps running after the Son has come, with a second people and a second destiny.

The picture at the top is that argument in one glance. Westminster covenant theology, 1689 Federalism, and progressive covenantalism meet in the middle. Scripture is organized by way of covenant. The circles part company over Abraham's sons, and over whether the new covenant is allowed to be new.

Peter Gentry and Stephen Wellum set the map out in *Kingdom through Covenant*, from the seminary where I studied. What follows is what a church does with the map. It is why the font waits on a profession, and why the pulpit does not preach a people of God beside Christ.

## What arrives in him

Adam stood under a command. Life was on the far side of obedience, and he fell. Call that a covenant of works and you have named the principle. You have not yet named the Bible. The first gospel word after the fall is not a new constitution. It is the promise of a seed. Noah's covenant keeps a world for that promise to enter. It preserves. It does not redeem.

Abraham is given a seed, a land, and a blessing for the nations. Paul will not let the seed become a crowd that merely includes Christ.

> "Now the promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, 'And to offsprings,' referring to many, but referring to one, 'And to your offspring,' who is Christ."
> ~ **Galatians 3:16**

The land is not cancelled, and it is not frozen as a strip of ground on a later calendar. Abraham is heir of the world. The inheritance widens until it is the new creation, the heir is Christ, and the heirs are those who are in him. The blessing to the nations is the point the promise was carrying.

Sinai is a covenant. Jeremiah and the writer to the Hebrews call it the old one, because the new one replaces it. Law, nation, priest, sacrifice, temple, and king are given there, and they are given as types. They are not the church's charter under a new typeface. Christ keeps the command Israel broke, offers the sacrifice the temple staged, and sits on the throne David was promised. The law and the old covenant belong together. We are not under that covenant. We are under its mediator.

The commands that belong to God's character, and to the creature made in his image, do not expire when Sinai's treaty does. They come to us from Christ and the apostles. They do not come as the terms on which a nation holds a strip of land.

One line in the left-hand circle of that diagram needs a steadier sentence than the graphic gives it. Grace is promised from the garden on. Genesis 3:15 is a promise. Abraham is a promise. David is a promise. What is not yet made, in those centuries, is the new covenant Jeremiah describes. Promised is not the same word as made. The circle that says grace was not promised says too little. The circle that says the covenant of grace was already made in the garden, and merely administered afterward, says too much.

## Why the font follows faith

Westminster reads one covenant of grace from the garden through Abraham into the church, administered once by circumcision and then by baptism. On that reading there is one covenant in Abraham, circumcision is its sign, and the children of believers wear the sign because they were born in the house. I have learned a great deal from the ministers who hold this. The learning does not move the font.

Jeremiah does not describe a mixed house that hopes its children will know the Lord later. He says they shall all know me, from the least to the greatest, and he ties that knowledge to forgiven iniquity. The new covenant community is the community of the forgiven. Baptism is the sign for those who profess that forgiveness.

We catechize our children because they are ours, and because the Lord's call is not closed to them. At Pentecost Peter says the promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off, as many as the Lord our God will call to himself. The call is the door. The house is the ordinary place of the call. The house is not a second way in.

1689 Federalism is kin to this reading. It is the account of the Second London Confession which says the covenant of grace was promised in the older Scriptures and established in Christ, and which keeps the covenant of circumcision as another covenant. I can live in that kinship. The confession is the standard. A map is a servant. Progressive covenantalism spends less of its strength on the two titles "covenant of works" and "covenant of grace," and more on letting each covenant keep the name the text gave it until Christ takes up what it said.

## The other progressive

![A timeline of dispensationalism, from Darby through progressive dispensationalism](/assets/img/site/dispensations.jpg)

The second picture is a family tree, and it is not ours. Classic dispensationalism, the revised line, the ultra line, and then progressive dispensationalism, the proposal associated with Craig Blaising and Darrell Bock. In that house the word progressive means the kingdom has begun and a future for national Israel is still ahead, with the church and Israel kept distinguishable to the end.

Progressive covenantalism does not take a room in that house. There is one people of God. Jew and Gentile are one new man, and the dividing wall is down. The promises are kept in Christ. Romans 11 is not a second covenant waiting offstage. It is mercy for Israel by the same gospel, into the same tree. Israel was really Israel. The prophets were not a code for a Gentile church that had replaced her. Christ is Israel in his own person, and his people are in him. A national chapter that still has to happen beside him would be another people, and another people is a smaller Christ.

## What the church owes this reading

Westminster was right that God deals with his people by covenant, and that Adam and Christ are the two federal heads. The dispensational teachers were right to refuse a sermon that never lets Israel be Israel. Both corrections fit inside the better account. They do not need their own systems once the covenants are allowed to arrive.

The test is the one this field-post already uses. A covenant that only resembles the new one will be trusted by people it has not forgiven. A diagram that keeps a second story running after the Son has come will be trusted by people Christ has already gathered. We baptize professors. We teach our children. We preach one gospel to Israel and to the nations. The new covenant is the one Jeremiah promised, and it is in force.

**Contra mundum. Code obscurum. Christ supreme.**
